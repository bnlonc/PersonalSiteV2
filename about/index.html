About me

Hi! 👋 I'm Ben.

I'm a software development engineer located in Seattle. I graduated from UC San Diego with a Bachelor of Science in Computer Science in 2023.

I was admitted as a National Merit Scholar and was awarded the Shores Scholarship, the highest-valued merit-based scholarship available at UCSD. While there, I was a student government officer for three years, Vice President of the Kendo Club at UCSD for one year, and President of the Kendo Club at UCSD for one year.

While in college, I interned at Amazon twice. After graduation, I returned to Amazon, where I currently develop AWS-based services that monitor and secure diverse third-party cloud environments.

I'm an Extra-class ham radio operator, electronics hobbyist, amateur guitar player, and music collector.

I have achieved the rank of kendo 2-dan and have had the privilege of practicing with:

  • San Diego Kendo Bu
  • The Kendo Club at the University of Washington

  • Seattle Kendo Kai

About my site

I built everything on this site other than the blog pages by hand in native HTML and CSS. I generate the blog pages using a lightweight content management system that I built in Python. It takes HTML templates and markdown files as inputs and generates HTML files for everything you see on the blog section. I statically host these output files on the site.

As you can probably tell, I'm not an artist, a web designer, or a front-end dev. My goal with this site is to create a fun and expressive space instead of serving something sterile and optimized for SEO or whatever. This site features no JavaScript, no telemetry, and no cookies, because I don't think any of those technologies are necessary for what I'm doing here. In fact, I'd much rather provide respite from the feeling of being constantly surveilled and tracked on the web. My server sends you the site content and nothing more.

In true small-web fashion, I self-host my site on a mini-desktop. It sits under a chair in my living room. That's a 92mm Noctua fan, for reference. Look upon my sheer computing might and despair.
